## I. PRELIMINARY: ESTABLISHING THE "RESIDUALS" THRESHOLD

**GOAL:** Lock Dana into the premise that she relied on "Unaided Memory" and establish the high complexity of the architecture in question to make her defense factually impossible.

**Q1.** Ms. Ruiz, as VP Engineering, you are testifying that Aspen’s new data-pipeline architecture (v4) was derived solely from your engineers’ "residuals"—that is, information retained in their unaided memory—following the NDA evaluation period. Is that correct?

**Q2.** And by "unaided," you mean no source code was copied from Harbor Peak, and no diagrams or documentation were retained at Aspen?

**Q3.** Let's define the architecture in question. You testified it involves a "tenant-partitioning *before* deduplication" workflow. That is a highly specific, non-standard sequential logic flow. Is it your position that a senior engineer can memorize this exact sequence and all its edge cases immediately after a review meeting without ever referring back to source material?

**Q4.** Isn't the truth that "sketching what that looks like" (as you directed in the 3/12 email) was actually an exercise in aided memory—reconstructing, from memory but guided by your own knowledge of the architecture, exactly how it *should* be built?

---

## II. THE "SMOKING GUN": THE 3/12 EMAIL TRAP
**GOAL:** Prove that Ms. Ruiz had conscious awareness that the concept was not hers to use without disclosure. Frame her instruction to Marco as "concealment of evidence," not IP hygiene.

**Q5.** Let's look at the email chain from **March 12th** regarding engineering tasks following your NDA sessions. (Show Exhibit A: 3/12 Email). In this message, you explicitly instructed Marco to immediately "sketch what that looks like in our scheduler." You did not order a feasibility study; you ordered immediate implementation of the concept.

**Q6.** Right below that instruction, you wrote: *"Don't document where it came from."* Is that accurate?

**Q7.** Ms. Ruiz, if your team was simply recalling high-level industry concepts protected by a residuals clause, why did you feel it necessary to explicitly order an engineer to **hide the source** of that concept?

**Q8.** (If defense claims "IP Hygiene"): You claim this is standard IP hygiene? I challenge you: Is it *standard practice* at Aspen to tell your engineers to obscure trade secrets because they learned them from a competitor during an NDA review? That instruction implies a consciousness that the source was sensitive or restricted, doesn't it?

---

## III. THE TIMELINE & MOTIVE: BOARD DECK & KDC
**GOAL:** Establish competitive intent by juxtaposing her technical "independence" with the business reality of "closing the gap."

**Q9.** Ms. Ruiz, you reviewed the Q2 Board Deck for Aspen. Paragraph 4 admits that Version 4 was built specifically to "close the gap" and match the market positioning of Harbor Peak's offering. Isn't that true?

**Q10.** In that same deck, we see that your team commissioned "Targeted Load Testing" (Invoice KDC-2026-118) via third parties only *weeks* after design. Why does your roadmap look like a sprint specifically timed to catch the exact weakness Harbor Peak announced during your NDA?

**Q11.** "Closing the gap" implies you are building what they built. If it were truly independent innovation, wouldn't that language in the Board Deck be misleading to the board members?

---

## IV. BAD FAITH DOCUMENTATION: THE DELETED CITATIONS
**GOAL:** Prove she tampered with her own documentation to make theft look like derivation. Highlight the "incident report" pivot as suspicious.

**Q12.** Now let's discuss your design memo from **April 2nd**. (Show Exhibit B). It notes that a citation referencing the "Harbor Peak approach" was deleted from the document on **April 1st**, *before* the memo was finalized and sent to stakeholders.

**Q13.** Prior to that deletion, wasn't the derivation clearly linked to what you learned during the Harbor Peak presentation?

**Q14.** But here is the critical question: On **April 5th**, immediately after deleting the citation, you sent a note to Legal asking: *"Is incident-report framing enough?"* (Show Exhibit C).

**Q15.** If you had truly deleted that reference because it was just generalized market knowledge, why would you need to ask your VP of Legal if your new justification was "enough"? You were literally asking for *permission* to re-frame the source of the code. Why not just say, "We found a bug in our old system and fixed it?"

## V. CLOSING THE WITNESS (Summary & Damning Inferences)

**Counsel:** Ms. Ruiz, the NDA residuals clause exists so you don't forget *general* ideas. It is not a shield that allows a Vice President to:
1.  Order engineers to **"don't document where it came from"** immediately after an evaluation;
2.  **Delete** references to the competitor in internal memos;
3.  And then ask Legal if they can **reframe** that deletion as "incident reports."

Do you have any defense for this sequence other than realizing you could not prove independent innovation?
