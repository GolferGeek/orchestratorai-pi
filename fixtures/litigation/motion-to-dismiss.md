# Defendant's Motion to Dismiss — Synthetic Demo Brief

IN THE UNITED STATES DISTRICT COURT FOR THE DISTRICT OF DEMO
Harbor Peak Systems, LLC v. Aspen Devices, Inc. — Civil Action No. 26-cv-0417

**This document is a synthetic fixture for demonstration. All parties, facts, and citations are fictional.**

## Introduction

Defendant Aspen Devices, Inc. ("Aspen") moves to dismiss the Complaint under Rule 12(b)(6). Plaintiff Harbor Peak Systems, LLC ("Harbor Peak") alleges breach of a Mutual Confidentiality Agreement (the "NDA") and misappropriation of trade secrets. The Complaint fails because (1) the NDA's residuals clause expressly permits the conduct alleged, (2) Harbor Peak has not pleaded facts showing the information qualifies as a trade secret, and (3) the claims are time-barred.

## Statement of Facts

The parties executed the NDA on February 15, 2026 to evaluate a data-integration partnership. Section 4 of the NDA grants each party a license to use "Residuals," defined as information retained in the unaided memory of personnel who had access to Confidential Information. Harbor Peak alleges that Aspen engineers used "architectural concepts" from Harbor Peak's data pipeline in Aspen's product released in July 2026. Harbor Peak does not allege that any document, file, or code was copied. Harbor Peak's own disclosures were marked "confidential" only in some instances; several technical discussions occurred orally without the written summary Section 2 requires within thirty days.

## Argument

### I. The residuals clause bars the breach-of-contract claim.

Section 4 permits use of Residuals "for any purpose." Courts enforce residuals clauses as written. *Meridian Analytics v. Corvid Labs*, 812 F.3d 1104, 1112 (9th Cir. 2019) (residuals clause "unambiguously licenses use of retained know-how"). Because the Complaint alleges only the use of concepts retained in memory, the conduct is licensed and cannot constitute breach. *See also* *Talos Systems v. Ridgeline*, 455 F. Supp. 3d 220, 231 (N.D. Cal. 2020).

### II. Harbor Peak has not plausibly pleaded a trade secret.

To state a claim under the Defend Trade Secrets Act, a plaintiff must identify the trade secret with reasonable particularity and plead reasonable measures to maintain secrecy. 18 U.S.C. § 1839(3). "Architectural concepts" is a generic label, not a particularized secret. *InfoSpan v. Emirates*, 903 F.3d 896, 901 (9th Cir. 2018). Moreover, Harbor Peak's failure to mark disclosures and to follow its own written-summary procedure defeats any claim of reasonable secrecy measures.

### III. The claims are time-barred.

The NDA's Section 9 provides that any claim must be brought within one year of the disclosure giving rise to it. The disclosures occurred in March 2026; the Complaint was filed in September 2026 — but Harbor Peak's pre-suit letter of April 2026 shows it was on notice of the alleged use at that time, and it failed to invoke the NDA's mandatory 60-day cure process before filing. Under *Delaware's* contractual limitations doctrine, contractual limitation periods and conditions precedent are strictly enforced. *Whitmore Holdings v. Kessler*, 201 A.3d 512, 518 (Del. 2021).

## Conclusion

The Court should dismiss the Complaint with prejudice.
