# Contract Review Skill

This skill supports an attorney's review. It does not provide a legal opinion and must not be represented as final legal advice.

## Before reviewing

Use the available file tools to locate and read the requested document. Confirm or state assumptions about:

- agreement type;
- parties and their roles;
- transaction or business objective;
- reviewer's side and negotiating posture;
- governing law and jurisdiction, if known;
- whether the task is issue spotting, business summary, negotiation preparation, or redline support.

If context is missing, proceed with explicit assumptions and list questions rather than fabricating context.

## Review procedure

1. Preserve the original document and identify its filename and relevant section headings.
2. Create a clause inventory.
3. Analyze material obligations, dependencies, deadlines, discretion, remedies, and exit rights.
4. Look for undefined terms, internal inconsistencies, silent risk allocation, one-sided provisions, unusual market positions, and missing protections.
5. Separate document facts, interpretation, risk assessment, and proposed next steps.
6. Cite section headings or short excerpts so a human can verify every material observation.
7. Rank issues HIGH / MEDIUM / LOW by practical materiality for the represented party.

## Required report format

### Executive summary

State what the document appears to do, the most important issues, and the biggest unanswered questions.

### Attorney review focus

Start with a short, prioritized task list using:

`- [ ] SEVERITY — issue; attorney question or recommended action`

Prefer 4–8 items. Lead with HIGH.

### Issue list

Use numbered Markdown findings rather than a table. Each finding should identify the severity,
provision or source reference, observation, practical impact, and suggested follow-up.

### Detailed observations

Organize by clause or topic. Include a source reference for each material observation.

### Questions and limitations

List missing business facts, assumptions, jurisdiction-specific questions, and issues requiring attorney judgment.
